Why your app looks AI-generated

A model reaches for the safest default every time, and the defaults are the same for everyone. That is why so many apps an AI helped build look alike. The good news is that most of it can be measured, and some of it is one knob away.

The tells UISandbox measures

Only what the computed style of your screen can show, the way Adrian Krebs measured 1,590 Show HN pages for design slop: from the DOM, with no model judging a screenshot.

When you open your app, UISandbox lists which of these are on your screen, and which of them a knob fixes.

What no knob can fix

Layout reads as generic too: a centred hero with a small badge above the headline, three or four identical cards each with an icon in a tinted square, numbered 1-2-3 steps on a landing page. These are not reliably measured on an app screen, where a row of figures is often the point, so UISandbox does not claim them as findings. It passes them to your agent as things to avoid when it builds the next screen.

See it on your own screen

npx uisandbox-mcp@latest open

Run it next to your dev server, then press 6: your own screen in six styles side by side. Pick one, change the font and the brand colour, and keep it as a design system your agent follows on every screen after this one.

Questions

Is Inter a bad font?

No. It is a good font that nearly every generated app now uses, so it reads as a default rather than a choice. Any font you picked on purpose fixes the tell.

Will my agent keep the new look?

Copy for your AI gives it the design system and how to apply it in one paste. On a UI kit the export adds a SKILL.md, so Claude Code, Cursor or Codex build the next screen in the kit's own classes.
